      Where the board senses motion

      Every board (squares), router and poked device (dots), and each link between them. The glow is where a person changes that link's signal, an estimate: strongest inside the thin outline (the 1st Fresnel zone, drawn to scale), fading outward as reflections carry the change around the room, and strong right next to either end. It pulses red while that link sees motion. Walls and furniture bend it. While there is motion, a red haze with a dashed ring shows where it most likely is: every link votes by how strongly it sees motion (yes here, or not here), and a link whose far end is only estimated votes more vaguely, so the ring widens rather than guess. Place the hub and your boards (drag the squares) and aim each one: drag the arrow by its square to the way it faces (its antenna hears best ahead, least through the wall behind). Routers are optional: place any you know; the others are guessed roughly, and the tracking leans on the boards. Pinch or scroll to zoom, drag empty space to pan: the view stays where you leave it.

      How to read this

      A link's motion zone depends only on its length and the channel, so once the dots are where things really are, the ovals are exact. Motion right next to the board or the device counts too, even outside the oval, because that end of the link is so close.

      The dashed ring around a dot means ackwave placed it from the signals: every placed board that hears it gives a distance, and together they give a spot. The faint area around it is how sure that is: one board gives only a ring, two a pair of spots, three or more a point. Walls make things look farther away, so it is a good start, not a survey. Drag a dot to fix it; Place automatically (in its row) or Auto-place hands it back.

      The "open air" estimate uses the free-space loss and the environment's fade rate. A link that is many dB weaker than that has walls or furniture in between.

            Network

            NetworkChSignal

            Pick your own router: Watch uses every access point with that name on the board's channel. To combine several routers (any names), tick their access points instead. The board listens on one channel, so set those routers to the same 2.4 GHz channel in their own settings. The board only listens and never joins, so no password is needed. The choice is saved on the board, which restarts and recalibrates (about 15 s).

            Detection

            Sensitivity is the one threshold the board lets you change (the slow, 4 s window); it is saved on the board. The rest are shown for reference; changing them needs new firmware.

            Buzzer

            Beeps: "di-di-DIIING" once motion has lasted 2 s (at most every 30 s), a sliding "dooooo" when it ends. Alarm: a siren while motion lasts. Every sound uses the volume set here. Saved on the board, so it keeps them unplugged from the PC too; hold BOOT for 5 s to put the board's settings back to the firmware's.

            Board LED

            Motion looks

            Status shows everything: motion in your colour, amber fading for 30 s after it, a quick purple flash for a blip too faint to count, a slow blue pulse while calibrating, yellow while looking for the router, and dark when the room is still. Motion only lights it for motion and nothing else. Saved on the board, so it stays the same when the board runs on its own.

            Wiring a board

            ESP32-S3: pins as printed on the board

            Buzzer: red (+) to GPIO 4, black (−) to GND. A passive piezo works either way round; an active buzzer needs + on GPIO 4. Then pick Beeps or Alarm above, and Test sounds.

            Button: the board's own BOOT button recalibrates (hold it 5 s to reset the settings). LED: the one on the board (GPIO 48), nothing to wire.

            A light (next firmware): never wire a light straight to a pin: a pin gives 3.3 V and a few milliamps. Put a switch module in between: IN to GPIO 5, VCC to 5V, GND to GND, and the light's own red and black wires through the module's output. For a USB or 5–24 V DC light (LED strip, lamp with a DC plug), a logic-level MOSFET module (e.g. one built on an IRLZ44N or AO3400). For anything plugged into the wall, don't wire mains yourself: use a smart plug and the "Light or smart device on motion" card below.

            Light output GPIO 5, through a switch module

            Board
            Light

            Switches a light wired to the board through a MOSFET or relay module (see Wiring a board). Each board decides from its own motion, so it works with no PC or Pi running. Saved on the board; another board's setting goes through the hub. Needs the firmware from 2026-11-21 or later.

            Phone notifications

            Uses the Pushover app (iPhone and Android). Make an application on pushover.net called ackwave, then paste its API token and your user key above. One message when the house goes from still to motion, then nothing until it has been quiet for the time above, so a busy evening is one message, not hundreds. It goes with the same boards as the light (Settings, boards that count). The keys are kept in dashboard.json with the board secret and never shown again.

            Light or smart device on motion

            Calls the first URL the moment motion starts and the second once the room has been still for the time above ("right away" switches it off as soon as the motion ends; brief blips then flick it on and off, and Voice Monkey may slow down very frequent calls). It only switches off a light it switched on. For an Amazon Smart Plug, make two Routine Trigger devices in Voice Monkey (e.g. "ackwave motion" and "ackwave still"), then two Alexa Routines: when each one is pressed, turn the plug on / off. Paste each device's trigger URL here. Any URL that switches something works (Home Assistant, IFTTT webhooks). The URLs hold secret tokens: they are kept in sessions/dashboard.json on this PC and never shown again.
