How to read this

The shaded oval around each link is its 1st Fresnel zone: the region where a person changes that link's signal the most. The dashed rings are the 2nd and 3rd zones, where motion still shows but more weakly. Its size depends only on the distance and the channel, so it's exact once the scale and positions are right.

The heatmap estimates where motion shows up most. It combines the Fresnel zones with bistatic scattering: a person close to either end of a link changes the signal far more than someone the same distance off the middle. Only links that pass ackwave's own limits count, and the router link, which decides alerts, carries most of the weight.

The signal estimate uses free-space loss, your environment's fade rate, and the walls you draw, crossed along the straight line. Real rooms add reflections, so expect it to be within about ±6 dB. Compare it with the live RSSI on ackwave's Links page and adjust the device's power (EIRP) until they match. After that, moving things around here predicts well.

Steps: add a picture, tap Scale and tap both ends of something you know the length of, drag the dots to where things really are, then draw the walls between them. Pinch or scroll to zoom, drag empty space to pan. Everything stays in this browser only.

Hardware numbers used
BoardESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 (N8R8 / N8R2): ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 module with PCB antenna
Board TX20.0 dBm at 802.11g 6 Mbps (Espressif WROOM-1 datasheet, typical). ackwave pokes at 6 Mbps.
Board RX–93 dBm sensitivity at 6 Mbps (Espressif ESP32-S3 datasheet, typical)
Board antenna0 dBi assumed. The antenna is the wavy gold trace at the end of the module, past the metal can. It changes a few dB with orientation: standing the board on its short edge, antenna end up, matches most routers' vertical antennas. Keep that end clear of metal, cables and the edge of a desk. The best test is live: turn the board while watching the router's dBm on ackwave's Links page.
ackwave limitsLinks weaker than –85 dBm are flagged weak (no motion beeps). APs and devices drop out of the rotation below –80 dBm.
DevicesEIRP presets are typical values for that class of device, not measured. Edit them to match what ackwave shows.
WallsTypical 2.4 GHz losses per crossing. Real walls vary, especially brick, concrete and coated glass.
