# Analysis of the 2026-11-13 recording

Recording `raw-2026-11-13_1143.log.gz`: 2026-11-13 11:43:12 to 2026-11-14 10:05:39 (22 h 22 min, 15.8 M lines). The file was copied while still recording, so the gzip stream ends without its trailer, as expected. Every pass read it as a stream, and one pass over the whole file takes about 20 s.

Links are named by node number only. Node 0 is the router (ACKs to the board's pokes). Nodes 1–2 are far access points that are never poked (`GATE far/weak`). Nodes 8, 9 and 10 are the poked devices; node 10 exists from 22:11:45 on. No network names or MAC addresses appear in this document.

## How this was measured

- **Events.** `events-2026-11-13_1143.log` was not on this machine, so the events come from replaying the raw log's control lines through the current `Recorder` (time from the `#T` markers, interpolated by board time). The replay gives the same counts as the original log: 265 motion starts, 33 brief blips. The Phase 2 diff against the original log is still to do.
- **Board verdict.** These are the board's own `STATE` lines. The host-built C detector (`build-host/score_lines`) was run over every CSI line from the last boot (14:25:36 to the end, 19.6 h). It reproduced all **424 of 424** board `STATE` changes at the same board timestamps. So the log holds everything the board's detector saw: no lines were lost (`STATS` lost counter flat all session), and the firmware matches the C code exactly.
- **Per-link verdicts.** These come from `web.py`'s `LinkDetector`, as the dashboard's per-link analysis would show them, each fed only its own link's ACKs.
- **Radars.** These come from the Home Assistant export `sessions/history.csv`: C4002 status/distances, C4001 range, CQRobot pulse peak, `erm_presence`. Per the sensor layout, none of them looks into the board–router zone itself, and other people live in the house. They are treated as evidence of movement nearby, not as ground truth, and every comparison is made against a random-time baseline.

## 3. The 33 brief blips: real or false?

**Short answer: mostly false, a few probably real.** About 5 are likely real small movements (bold in the table): two or three radars saw movement at the same moment, or the blip sits right next to a sustained period. About 20 have nothing in support and look like the short window's noise tail. The rest can't be told apart.

### Evidence that most are not motion in the zone

1. **No step in the CSI.** For each blip, the router link's normalised amplitude shape was compared 1.5–4 s before and after. The median change is 0.38× the packet-to-packet noise, against 0.81× at the start of real (long-window) motion: the shape goes back to where it was. A person stepping into or out of the zone normally leaves a lasting change.
2. **No glitch, no RSSI step.** The shape jump at each blip (median 0.14) equals the 99th percentile of the minute before (0.14); the glitch limit is 0.4. The router RSSI around blips changes by −0.01 dB median (at most 0.7 dB).
3. **No link to router scans.** The scan rhythm in this recording is confirmed: 36 pairs of router ACK gaps (100 ms or more) lie 1202 ± 5 s (20 min 2 s) apart. The blips' distance from that rhythm is uniform (median 305 s, the most it can be is 601 s; 2 fall within 30 s, 1.6 would by chance). Only 3 blips are within 5 s of any ACK gap of 100 ms or more.
4. **Device links don't confirm them.** In random 6 s windows with no board motion, the devices' own detectors flag motion this often: link 8 2%, link 9 9%, link 10 10%. At blips: link 8 1/33, link 9 6/33, link 10 3/21. That is chance level, so the device links neither confirm nor rule out a blip.
5. **The short window spikes by itself in quiet time.** In 18.5 quiet hours of the last boot, the router's short window crossed ×2.8 another **101 times** while the long window was at ×1.2 or below, so the gate blocked them. The 33 blips are the spikes that happened while the long window sat just over ×1.2. In quiet time, the router's short ratio has its 99.9th percentile at ×2.51 and its 99.99th at ×3.52.
6. **ACK gaps make spikes far more likely** (quiet time, last boot, per 100 ms):

   | Longest router ACK gap in the last 1 s | P(short ≥ ×2.8) |
   |---|---|
   | < 30 ms | 0.00002 |
   | 30–45 ms | 0.0005 |
   | 45–60 ms | 0.0012 |
   | 60–80 ms | 0.0019 |
   | ≥ 80 ms | 0.0050 |

   The router ACK rate shows the same thing: P = 0.0001 at ≥ 95 ACK/s, 0.003 at 70–85 and 0.006 below 70. At the blips, the router answered 91 ACKs in the second before (median), against 95 in quiet time. Six blips came after 81 or fewer ACKs, down to 65.

   Two mechanisms are possible. (a) The detector's input filters are updated per packet with time-based weights: after a gap, the 32 ms low-pass takes one fresh, noisy packet almost whole, and the 0.5 s window averages fewer packets. (b) Something such as interference both drops ACKs and disturbs the CSI. Gaps are a contributor, not the whole cause: the largest gap at a blip (median 60 ms) is no larger than the largest in the minute before (median 71 ms).

### Evidence that some are real

From the Home Assistant export: 12 of 33 blips fall within ±10 s of C4002 "Motion", against 22% (about 7) expected by chance, binomial p ≈ 0.04. Four fall at a C4001 range change (4% chance, p ≈ 0.04). Several tests were run, so these p-values are weak. Five blips had two or three radars active at once, and 7 fall within 30 s of a sustained Wi-Fi period. Those are probably small real movements, most likely from the room behind the zone or the hall, which the radars cover. They are marked in bold.

## 5. The "9 min 12 s" period at 14:25:21 spans a restart

What happened:

- **14:24:36** (2:24 PM CST; you made this switch from the PC) The board ran a network scan (`NETS`, 22 networks), then got `USE` for another saved network (not the usual one) and restarted (`rst:0xc RTC_SW_CPU_RST`). It came up on that network's AP on **channel 3** (RSSI ≈ −57).
- **14:24:55** Calibrated on that link: quiet level 0.0505.
- **14:25:05–14:25:12** and **14:25:21** Motion on that link.
- **14:25:36** Switched back (`NETWORK,saved`), restarted again, and came up on channel 6 with the usual router.
- **14:26:00** Calibrated: quiet level 0.0251 (fast phase), tracking from 14:27:56 at 0.0240.

**`STATE` after the 14:26:00 calibration:** none until board time 512 s. The board stayed **still from 14:26:00 to 14:34:17**. It then reported motion at 14:34:17 and still at 14:34:33.

A restarted board starts in "still" and only prints `STATE` on a change, so no "still" line ever closed the 14:25:21 motion. `Recorder` kept the period open across the restart and ended it with the next "still", at 14:34:33. It also swallowed the real 14:34:17 start, because a period was already open. The truth is 14:25:21–14:25:36 (about 15 s, cut short by the restart, on the other network), then nothing, then 14:34:17–14:34:33 (16 s). The artefact added 8 min 41 s to the totals and one false "> 60 s" period.

The board's own detector behaves correctly here: on any recalibration its floor drops to 0, `md_eval` clears the motion flags and prints `STATE,still`. The missing close is only in the recorder. A recalibration with motion open didn't happen in this recording, but the same defensive close belongs there (Phase 2).

## 6. First calibration: 0.042, against 0.021 later

The board booted about 29 s before the recording started; the log begins at board time 29.6 s. So the 10 s boot collect itself is not in the raw data. What is there:

- The first `CALIB` line (board 30.2 s, `fast,boot`) has floor **0.0420**. This is what the recorder printed ("Calibrated (quiet level 0.0420)").
- The board's first `STATE` is `still` at 30.36 s, with long-window score 0.050. So the board was in **motion during or right after the boot calibration**, with the long window at about 2.4× the later quiet level.
- In the fast phase the floor fell quickly: 0.0420 → 0.0312 (11:43:32) → 0.0244 (11:43:37) → 0.0236 when tracking began (11:44:52). Fast phase lets the floor rise only to 1.5× its first value, but fall freely, so it corrected itself within about 25 s.
- The manual recalibration at 13:27:34 gave 0.0210, tracking 0.0212. After the restart, the level settled at 0.024–0.027.

**Conclusion:** yes, someone was moving during the boot calibration, most likely while setting up the board. The 10th percentile of the 10 s collect was raised about 75%, and the detector corrected it by itself within half a minute. Little harm was done: the first periods (11:43:12, 11:43:52) were detected even against the raised floor. The misleading part is the event log, which only ever shows the first value (improvement #4).

## 9. Ranked bugs and improvements

### Bugs

1. **The recorder doesn't close a motion period when the board restarts.** *(Confirmed, §5.)* It produced the 9 min 12 s artefact, put 8 min 41 s too much into the totals and swallowed a real start. **Fix:** in `Recorder.feed()`, detect a restart (board timestamp going back by more than 1 s, as `gui.State` already does, or a `CALIB` `wait`/`collect` line with reason `boot`) and close the open period at the last time seen before the restart. Also close it on any recalibration `CALIB wait/collect`; the board sends `STATE,still` there itself, so this is only a safety net. Log "Board restarted" as its own line. Tests: restart while in motion, and recalibration while in motion.
2. **The dashboard's session timeline, counts and "last motion" come from the PC's detector, not the board's verdict.** `gui.State.hist_motion` is fed by the PC-side `MotionDetector`, which uses the floor from the board's `CALIB` lines. Those are printed every 5 s, while the board updates its floor every second. Near the thresholds the two disagree (§8: +5 / −1 periods and +46 s over 19.6 h; small but visible), so the GUI/web summary can differ from the events log and the board's LED. **Fix:** build `hist_motion` and the counts from the board's `STATE` lines, which `board_states` already collects. Keep the PC detector for drawing scores. No detector maths changes. Test: a feed where the PC detector and `STATE` disagree, and the timeline must follow `STATE`.
3. **The events log gives a misleading quiet level.** Only the first fast-phase floor is logged ("0.0420"), never the settled one. **Fix:** also log when tracking starts ("quiet level settled at 0.0236"). If the floor fell by more than 30% during the fast phase, add "(it was moving during calibration)". Test with a fake `CALIB` sequence.
4. **The recorder mislabels the board's network scan.** A network scan (`NETS`) recalibrates with reason `channel` and is logged as "router changed channel". Switching networks isn't logged at all. **Fix:** log "Network scan (board paused)" when a `NET` or `NETS` line arrives around the recalibration. Log "Board switched network (channel N)" on `NETWORK,saved`/`chosen` + restart, without the name, so the events log stays shareable.

### Improvements, by expected value

5. **Short-window blips (§3).** About two thirds are probably false, and their odds rise sharply after ACK gaps. Options, all **detector maths, so they need your approval** and an offline test on this log first (how many of the 33 go away, and whether the 5 radar-backed ones survive):
   a. Short window may start motion only if the router link had no ACK gap over about 50 ms in the last 0.5 s, or at least about 80% of its usual packet count.
   b. Short window must stay over ×2.8 for at least 2 evaluations, 200 ms apart.
   c. Raise `short_gate` from ×1.2 to ×1.3. This is the simplest, but it would also drop some real starts.

   Display-only alternative, with no maths change: keep blips out of "last motion" and the Home view, and show them as faint ticks (the GUI already lists them apart).
6. **Per-link verdicts at about 31 ACK/s are too noisy to use as evidence** (link 10: 279 night starts; link 9: 9% of random quiet windows). **Fix** (display only): raise `LINK_MIN_RATE`/`LINK_OK_RATE` from 15/20 to about 40/45 ACK/s. Or show a device link's motion only when its long window, not just its short one, crosses. Recommend **at most 2 devices** for per-link use: the device budget is fixed, and the USB link has no headroom for more lines.
7. **Night long-window detections sit at the threshold.** Eleven of 12 peak at ×1.40–1.55, and only 1 was seen by every link. This matches 11-12/13. The `SENS` setting already exists. A "night sensitivity" schedule, or showing the peak strength next to each event, would let you judge them. No change to the default.
8. **`REFUSED,bad-command` doesn't say which command** (one at 11:43:12, right after connecting). **Fix:** have the firmware echo the first ~16 characters (`REFUSED,bad-command,<text>`, keeping the old form parseable), and log refused commands on the PC (Debug page, Phase 3). The firmware change needs a reflash; it's optional.
9. **Watch item: router-scan gaps are shorter here** (about 110 ms, against about 175 ms on 11-09), close to the 100 ms `scan_gap_us` limit. Some rhythm scans fell under 100 ms and weren't seen as gaps. None of them caused a blip or a false start in this recording, so nothing needs changing yet.
10. **Calibration warning on the Home page.** When the fast phase falls a lot (§6), say "calibrated while something moved, settled after 25 s" instead of nothing.
